It was a Monday morning — I remember it clearly. We had just come off an intense weekend: two simultaneous public tenders, one for a preschool and another for a sports center. Everyone at the firm had been working around the clock.

That day, while my boss was away on a site visit, I was in charge of something seemingly simple: delivering the sealed envelopes with the proposals and budgets to their respective city councils.

## But I switched them. The wrong envelopes went to the wrong places.

I realized the mistake when filing our internal copies. And I panicked. Instead of looking for a solution, I spent hours circling around guilt and excuses, trying to figure out how I had messed up.

Today I know the most important thing in a crisis is this:

Acknowledge that there’s a problem.

Stay calm.

And above all, don’t let panic drive you further from the solution.

When my boss returned, he didn’t yell. He taught. Within an hour, we had the situation under control. Once the fire was out, we sat down to analyze what happened, how it happened, and how to make sure it didn’t happen again.

That was my first real-life lesson in crisis management.

What did I learn from that experience?

Here are some principles I still apply today:

Acknowledge the incident immediately. Denial or finger-pointing only delays resolution.

Stay calm. Stress clouds judgment. Breathe, pause, and assess.

Focus on clarity. Understand what happened, the impact, and what resources are available.

Inform the right people. You don’t need the perfect solution — owning up early shows maturity.

Act fast but wisely. Don’t rush, but don’t freeze either.

Learn from the mistake. A well-handled crisis is unforgettable — and unrepeatable.

Because not every Monday starts right. But every one has something to teach us.
