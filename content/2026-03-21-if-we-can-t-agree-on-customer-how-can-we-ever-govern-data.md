## Why Your Business Glossary Deserves a Seat at the Grown-Up Table

But let's be honest. Most governance programs crash before takeoff.

Not because we lack tools, committees, or ambition. But because nobody actually agrees on what anything means.

Think about it. Ask five people across one company what "revenue" means and you will get eight answers.

Finance quotes IFRS. Sales talks about bookings. Marketing says MQL conversions (obviously). And Product? "Depends. Do you mean by customer or by feature?"

That, my friends, is semantic chaos.

And hiding right in the middle of it all is the most underrated artifact in data governance: the humble business glossary.

## The Underdog of Governance

A business glossary often gets dismissed as boring documentation work.

"We'll define the terms later," someone says. Or: "We already have definitions in Confluence. Somewhere. I think."

Spoiler: nobody ever finds them.

But this so-called "documentation overhead" is actually the foundation. Without shared language, everything else (policies, data quality rules, reporting standards) spends its energy negotiating meaning instead of enforcing truth.

As someone once told me (Eddy in Holland) and yes, I will be quoting you in every conversation from now on:

"Shared language is the first layer of governance. If people can't point to where meaning is defined, every downstream control ends up negotiating interpretation instead of enforcing rules."

The Bigger the Company, the Bigger the Babel

As organizations grow, so does their vocabulary confusion. Each business unit evolves like a different species, with its own metrics, its own definitions, its own version of truth.

Marketing says "active user" clicked an email last month. Product says it is anyone who logged in. Finance says it is only those who paid.

Three definitions. One metric. Zero alignment.

So when leadership asks for "monthly active users," your governance meeting suddenly turns into a United Nations summit where everyone speaks English but nobody means the same thing by it.

## Why Glossaries Fail (and How Not to Be That Story)

When you introduce a glossary, you quickly realize that definitions are political. Everyone guards their terminology like national heritage.

Say "We need to standardize customer" and someone will smile, nod... and quietly keep using their own definition.

That is why many glossary efforts fail. Not because the idea is flawed, but because it is perceived as imposed. Governance is not about control. It is about alignment. You are not erasing anyone's language. You are creating a translation layer so teams can actually understand each other.

It is not about forcing a dictionary. It is about holding a conversation.

## Building the Glossary People Actually Want to Use

So what makes a glossary stick? Co-creation. Simple as that.

It should reflect how the business actually works, then bring harmony. You cannot email out a template and call that collaboration. That is archaeology, not governance.

Try this instead. Hold definition workshops, debate meaning, and remember that snacks help diplomacy. Visualize overlaps using data catalogs and lineage tools to make the mess visible. Add examples, because a definition without context is like a recipe without ingredients. And keep it alive, because glossaries evolve and should be treated as living assets, not PDFs filed under "Q3 Initiatives." (because everybody hates Q1)

When done well, teams start asking: "Wait, what is our agreed definition of churn again?" That is governance gold.

## Communication Is the Work

Here is the secret: glossary work is communication work.

It is talking to every team, collecting worldviews, and mediating semantic debates over coffee. Or stronger drinks. No judgment.

It is part anthropology, part therapy, part translation service. But done right, it builds trust. The moment when people stop questioning the data because they finally understand what it means.

## The Magic Downstream Effect

Once your shared language exists, the downstream effects are significant.

Data quality rules become measurable. Policies stop tripping over grey areas. Reports align across functions. Decisions move faster because definitions are no longer up for debate.

That is the beauty of a glossary. It does not make governance heavier. It makes it lighter. You are not building walls. You are laying a shared floor.

## Real Talk: It Is Not a Magic Wand

A glossary will not fix broken pipelines, legacy data, or that secret Excel file someone swore they deleted.

But it will give you structure in the chaos. Think of it as your organization's Rosetta Stone: translating different business dialects into one trusted language.

## Governance Is a Story About Meaning

At its core, governance is not about control. It is about shared meaning. And meaning is built through conversation.

A business glossary is not just a repository of definitions. It is a mirror of how knowledge flows, who owns it, and where trust begins. It is the first shared contract between business and data teams.

If governance is your house, the glossary is your foundation. You cannot build a data-quality penthouse on semantic quicksand. 🏗️

TL;DR ... Because Someone Always Says "Too Long, Didn't Read"

Data chaos starts when meaning fractures. A business glossary fixes that by aligning definitions. Forcing it fails; co-creating it wins. Communication is governance. Once language is shared, everything downstream flows smoother.

So the next time someone says "Do we really need a business glossary?" smile kindly. Then ask them to define revenue.

You will have your answer in five conflicting definitions.
