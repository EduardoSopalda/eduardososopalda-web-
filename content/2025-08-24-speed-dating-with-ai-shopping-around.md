“Do I have something in my teeth?”

If AI were a first date, that’s how it would start—slightly awkward, wildly promising, and full of surprises. Welcome to Speed Dating with AI: each week I’ll “meet” a different AI, see what it’s great at, where it falls short, and whether it deserves a second date.

## What is AI?

## Artificial Intelligence is not magic. It’s data + algorithms + repeated learning cycles to perform tasks that usually require human intelligence.

Training vs. Inference (a two-phase relationship)

Training: the “gym phase.” The model learns from large datasets (costly, compute-heavy).

Inference: the “real-world phase.” The trained model answers, predicts, generates—fast and (ideally) efficient.

## The Different Faces of AI

AI isn’t one thing; it’s a family of approaches:

Machine Learning (ML): learns patterns to predict (fraud, churn, recommendations).

Natural Language Processing (NLP): understands/generates language (chatbots, summarization).

Computer Vision: interprets images/video (quality control, medical imaging).

Robotics & Autonomous Systems: acts in the physical world (warehouse robots, self-driving).

Generative AI: creates new text, images, audio, video, or code.

What’s striking is that many of the AIs we already use daily—sometimes without even realizing—fall under these categories. From recommendation engines in streaming platforms, to voice assistants on our phones, to fraud detection running silently in the background, AI is already woven into our everyday lives.

## From First Wave to Second Wave

Wave 1: LLMs went mainstream (ChatGPT, Copilot) and everyone “dipped a toe.”

Wave 2 (now): targeted adoption—teams pick specific use cases tied to data readiness, compliance, and ROI. Less hype, more fit-for-purpose.

And remember: everyone wants to date a surfer. If you missed the first wave, educate yourself and get ready for the second — because this one is about real, sustainable adoption.

## The Future: Bubble or Backbone?

Some predict the AI bubble will deflate—training costs, maintenance, and unclear ROI will force a correction. Others point to a reinforcing loop: AI drives demand for better chips, tooling, and efficiency (think NVIDIA & co.), which then makes AI more sustainable and useful.

My take: we’re in AI’s adolescence—brilliant, moody, and boundary-testing. That’s exactly why governance, measurement, and ethics matter now.
